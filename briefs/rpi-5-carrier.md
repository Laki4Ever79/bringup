# Brief: carrier for the Raspberry Pi 5 (brain)

## What we want
A HAT for the Raspberry Pi 5, for a robot: it sits on top of the Raspberry Pi 5, plugged onto its 40-pin header, and feeds it 5 V from the robot's battery through that header. The Raspberry Pi 5 is bought, not designed here; this board is designed around it. The motor driver and real-time control stay on the robot's main board.

## Must have
- **The Raspberry Pi 5's own outline and holes, so the two stack:** 85 × 56 mm, with 4 Ø2.7 mm mounting holes at (3.5, 3.5), (61.5, 3.5), (3.5, 52.5), (61.5, 52.5) mm from the lower-left corner, 58 × 49 mm between centres (https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rpi5/raspberry-pi-5-mechanical-drawing.pdf, top view: Ø2.7 holes, 3.5 from the left and bottom edges, 58 and 49 between centres), seen from above as the Raspberry Pi 5 is. The HAT stands on 16 mm spacers on those holes (https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/m2-hat-plus/, 'supplied with a 16mm stacking header and threaded spacers, so it can be fitted to a Raspberry Pi 5 with the Raspberry Pi Active Cooler in place').
- **A 2 × 20 female stacking header on the underside, 2.54 mm pitch,** centred at (32.5, 52.5) mm in the same frame, so it mates with the Raspberry Pi 5's header pins, which point up from its top face; pin 1 at the lower-left of the header (https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rpi5/raspberry-pi-5-mechanical-drawing.pdf, top view: header centre 29 right of the left holes (3.5 + 29), on the holes' row).
- **5 V supply: 5 A at 5.1 V** from the robot's GENSACE-GEA22002S20X6 2S 2200 mAh 20C LiPo XT60 (6–8.4 V), so a buck converter, into header pins 2 and 4 (5V), ground on pins 6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34 and 39. Sized from the Raspberry Pi 5's 5 A peak (https://github.com/raspberrypi/documentation/blob/master/documentation/asciidoc/computers/raspberry-pi/power-supplies.adoc, table row Raspberry Pi 5: recommended PSU current capacity 5.0A): that is the vendor's recommended supply and already includes its headroom, so no margin on top.
- **The link to the main board is that board's own USB** (the MCU board's native USB (CDC-ACM), one cable per board through the robot's USB-C hub; it shows up on the Raspberry Pi 5 as /dev/ttyACM0: https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32c3/api-guides/usb-serial-jtag-console.html, Uploading the Application, note: the USB Serial/JTAG Controller's serial port appears 'as /dev/ttyACM* on Linux'). It is a cable through the robot's USB-C hub, not a signal on this board: the carrier carries no link pins. The first board to enumerate gets ttyACM0; with several MCU boards, pin each by its USB serial number (a udev rule or /dev/serial/by-id) rather than by order.
- **No 5 V on any other header pin.** The Raspberry Pi 5's GPIO run at 3.3 V and aren't 5 V tolerant (https://github.com/raspberrypi/documentation/blob/master/documentation/asciidoc/computers/raspberry-pi/gpio-on-raspberry-pi.adoc, 'GPIO pins operate at 3.3 V and aren't 5 V tolerant').

## Out of scope
- The motor driver, the sensors and the robot's real-time control: they stay on the main board.
- A display, a fan, or anything on the Raspberry Pi 5's USB, Ethernet or camera ports.
